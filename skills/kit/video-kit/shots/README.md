# Shots that are not drawn by the page: base, transformation, production

The cinematic engine (`template/cinematic.js`) draws text, cards, diagrams, phones and illustrations from HTML: always available, never realistic. A shot that must look **real** (an object, a place, a light, a material, a person) is made in three ways that are used together, none of them a library of finished scenes:

1. **A base from a catalogue**, found by description and downloaded only if kept: 3D models, HDRI lights and PBR textures in `asset-research/` (CC0, Poly Haven: about 520 models, 1000 lights, 870 textures); free music in `music-research/`; a free footage through `footage-mirror.yml` (Internet Archive, Wikimedia Commons, licence written by whoever asks).
2. **A transformation, written for the film**: Blender, headless, on the CPU, with the functions of `shots/blender/kit.py` (import an asset, set it on the floor, bring it to the palette of the film, stage it with a light rig or an HDRI, a camera with depth of field and a move, a beam, render PNG frames) and, for any shot of any origin, `video-kit transform` (grade, bloom, grain, vignette, speed, crop: the same look on every shot, so that a render, a real footage and a clip from a generator read as one picture); a real footage gets our elements over it with `track.py`, `render --transparent` and `assemble --clips`.
3. **A production**, for what exists nowhere: the shapes, the movements and the graphics of the subject, written as a Blender script from the brief (`shots/blender/examples/product.py` makes a shape from nothing) or drawn by the page.

What none of these makes is a **photographic person or place**: that takes a real footage or a video generator of the user's (`video-kit shotlist` writes the prompts). A drawn, rendered or generated shot is never presented as a recording.

| Need | Tool | What it gives | What it costs / limits |

## Where a film gets its 3D: every scene is asked, not one hero object

The question is asked of **each scene of the script**, not once for the film: *what real thing would make this scene stronger, and does it fit in the time?* A scene about a device is a 3D device with the interface on its screen (`kit.phone`); a scene about a place or a planet is a lit sphere or a set; a scene about an object is the object from the catalogue; a scene that is a diagram, a number or a sentence stays on the page, and that is a choice, not an omission. Write the answer for every scene in `script.md` (`html`, `blender`, `footage`, `generated`) and say why when a scene stays on the page.

- **An interface on a 3D device**: render the interface page alone, at the rate of the 3D render, as PNG frames (a `?screen=` mode in the page: only the screen, transparent around it; `render page.html?screen=x frames/ --fps=12 --seconds=4.6 --width=600 --height=1180 --transparent`), number them from `0001`, and give the pattern to `kit.phone('screens/%04d.png', frames=N)`: the screen shows the real page, frame by frame, on a body of rounded metal that reflects an HDRI. Hide the soft boxes from the reflections (`obj.visible_glossy = False` for the lights) and turn the HDRI until no bright patch sits on the glass (look at 3 stills). `docs/video-vision/shots/phone_hero.py` is an example; `kit.reset(transparent=True)` (or `--transparent 1`) keeps the alpha so that the shot lies over the page.
- **A planet or a place**: `kit.planet('earth'|'mars', radius, location, spin, tilt, frames)` makes a sphere from procedural textures (no image of a real planet: say « rendu »), `kit.sun(direction, strength)` lights it. To lay it exactly where a page draws a disc, use an orthographic camera (`ortho_scale` 19.2 for 1920 px: 100 px to the unit), restrict the render to the part of the frame that holds it (`render.use_border`), and render the page without its disc (a `?noplanets=1` mode). `docs/video-vision/shots/planets_hero.py` is an example. A still screenshot on a 3D phone works too: repeat the image as the numbered sequence (symlinks) and give it to `kit.phone`.
- **One look for the whole film**: `assemble ... --grade=cinematic --bloom=0.15 --grain=0.02 --vignette=0.25` applies the same grade to everything (drawn pages, renders, footage) in the single encode. Judge it on stills before and after: on flat interface scenes a strong grade changes the identity of the film (a lifted black turns purple); keep it light, or only the vignette and the grain.
- **The budget is time, and it is planned**: a 1280x720 frame costs 10 to 25 s on the CPU, a film is 12 frames a second brought to 30 by `transform --interpolate`. 4 s of 3D is about 50 frames, 10 to 20 minutes. Count the seconds of 3D the quality allows (extra: as many scenes as fit, a few minutes each; moyen: none) before starting, render each shot in the background with a script that skips the frames already made (`kit.render` does), and never let the whole film wait for one shot.

## Make it a scene, not an object (what separates a render from a shot)

A shape on a black floor is a technical demo. A **set** is what reads as a film: real props of a believable scale (`asset-research`: tables, books, plants, lamps, eyewear from Poly Haven, CC0), an interior light that explains where the picture is, **dimmed for the camera and kept for the lighting** (see `examples/desk.py`: the room lights the scene, the camera sees it twelve times darker, out of focus), a warm practical light and one accent rim, a shallow depth of field, and a camera that is **blocked**: it starts on the subject, travels, and pulls its focus to the next thing the script talks about. Look at stills first. The invented object (here a phone: no modern one in the catalogue) is the only thing allowed to be made up, and it is said. Render the words in a **second, transparent layer** so that they stay above the shot, with a soft gradient under them where the ground is bright; sound is **designed** (`src/sfx.py`: whooshes under the camera moves, plucks, pings, a room tone, a riser and a boom, all synthesised) and mixed under the voice; one `--grade` on the whole.

## The flow

1. In the script (stage 2), mark each scene with its tool: `html`, `blender`, `footage` or `generated`. `video-kit shotlist storyboard.json out/` writes `shotlist.md` (what to produce, the prompts for a generator) and `clips.json`, and **refuses** a footage without its licence.
2. Make each shot. A 3D shot: look for a base (`node asset-research/src/cli.js search "..." --kind=model`, then `fetch`), write the script of the shot with `kit.py` (see `examples/product.py`), look at ONE frame first (`--still 40 --width 640 --height 360 --samples 16`), then render. A real footage: `python3 video-kit/src/track.py footage.mp4 track.json --bbox=x,y,w,h`, and a page that reads `track.json` and renders with `--transparent`. Then give every shot the same look: `node video-kit/src/cli.js transform in out.mp4 --grade=cinematic --bloom=0.3 --grain=0.04 --vignette=0.3`.
3. Lay them: `node video-kit/src/cli.js assemble frames/ film.mp4 --audio=music.wav --voice=vo.wav --duck=light --clips=clips.json`. A clip with an alpha channel goes over the picture, an opaque one replaces it, each with a short cross-fade.
4. Critique on the contact sheet as always (`docs/DIRECTOR-PLAYBOOK.md`), then say in the report which shots are real footage, which are 3D, which are generated, which are drawn.

## Honest rules

- A drawn or rendered or generated shot is never presented as a recording. Nothing is added to a real footage that changes what it shows (a profile is an element *over* it, labelled as an illustration of the idea).
- A real footage keeps its source, its licence and the credit it asks for, in `brief.md` and in the film if it asks. The right to the image of people is not settled by the licence of the file.
- What does not work here is said, not faked: no GPU (Blender is slow), no access to most footage sites, no video generator of ours.
